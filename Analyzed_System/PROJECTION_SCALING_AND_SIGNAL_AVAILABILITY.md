**Purpose:** Resolve what can be justified now about the weighted projection metric and RBF regressor, and identify the missing physical/sensing decisions that prevent numerical choices or an adaptive-law proof.

## 2. Projection metric: dimensional and mathematical requirements

The acceleration residual is
\[
r_q=\begin{bmatrix}r_x\\r_\theta\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
b_q=\begin{bmatrix}b_x^F\\b_\theta^F\end{bmatrix},
\]
where the superscript (F) distinguishes the force-to-acceleration direction from cart viscous damping. The units are (r_x\,[\mathrm{m/s^2}]), (r_\theta\,[\mathrm{rad/s^2}]), (b_x^F\,[\mathrm{(m/s^2)/N}]), and (b_\theta^F\,[\mathrm{(rad/s^2)/N}]).

Choose strictly positive characteristic acceleration scales (a_s) and \(\alpha_s\), and define
\[
S=\operatorname{diag}(a_s^{-2},\alpha_s^{-2}).
\]
The objective
\[
J(\delta)=\frac{(r_x-b_x^F\delta)^2}{a_s^2}
+\frac{(r_\theta-b_\theta^F\delta)^2}{\alpha_s^2}
\]
is dimensionless. Its unique minimiser, provided \(b_q^TSb_q>0\), is
\[
\delta_m=
\frac{b_x^F r_x/a_s^2+b_\theta^F r_\theta/\alpha_s^2}
{(b_x^F)^2/a_s^2+(b_\theta^F)^2/\alpha_s^2}.
\]
The result has units of newtons. The denominator has units \(\mathrm{N^{-2}}\); consequently a uniform lower bound \(b_q^TSb_q\ge\beta_b>0\) has \(\beta_b\) in \(\mathrm{N^{-2}}\).

This establishes dimensional consistency, not a preferred numerical metric. The ratio between \(a_s\) and \(\alpha_s\) determines the relative penalty assigned to cart- and angular-acceleration mismatch. A change in this ratio can change \(\delta_m\), the unmatched residual, and the reported RBF compensation. It is therefore a modelling/design choice and must be justified before performance comparisons.

## 3. Defensible scale-selection procedure

The scales must be selected from a declared admissible operating envelope, not fitted to a favourable simulation or chosen to make the projection denominator large.

1. Freeze a provisional *physical* envelope: bounds on cart position/velocity, pendulum angle/angular velocity, admissible parameter values and rates, and external disturbances. These bounds must be justified by the study's intended operating domain and safety constraints.
2. On that envelope, calculate or conservatively bound the acceleration magnitudes generated by the model and the declared force/disturbance limits. Keep cart and angular acceleration units separate.
3. Set \(a_s\) and \(\alpha_s\) as declared characteristic residual scales tied to those bounds (for example, specified fractions of the admissible cart and angular acceleration ranges). Record the fractions and rationale before comparing controllers.
4. Evaluate \(b_q^TSb_q\) over the complete parameter/angle envelope. A grid minimum is a numerical diagnostic, not a proof of a continuous-domain lower bound. Use interval bounds, analytic inequalities, or a conservative validated enclosure if a formal \(\beta_b\) is needed.
5. Repeat the residual decomposition and closed-loop evaluation over a predeclared sensitivity set of scale ratios. Report whether conclusions change materially. Do not select the ratio post hoc by minimising the observed tracking error.

The physical parameter register currently has TBD values for \(M,m,l,I_c,b_x,b_\theta\), force limit and actuator characteristics. The state and disturbance envelope is also not frozen. Therefore this repository does **not** yet contain enough information to choose defensible numerical values of \(a_s\), \(\alpha_s\), or \(\beta_b\). Existing values 0.08 m/s² and 0.10 rad/s² remain provisional examples only and must not be treated as accepted design constants.

## 4. Actual versus estimated input direction

The exact projection in the companion derivation uses the true direction
\[
b_q(X,p)=\frac{1}{D}
\begin{bmatrix}I\\-ml\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}.
\]
If the varying mass properties and inertia are not measured online, a realizable controller cannot use this true direction. The analysis must distinguish:

- **Analysis quantity:** \(b_q(X,p)\), calculated from the true plant parameters to define the exact decomposition.
- **Controller quantity:** \(\hat b_q(X,\hat p)\), calculated only from measured or estimated parameter values.
- **Direction error:** \(\tilde b_q=\hat b_q-b_q\), whose effect on the estimated scalar force and the resulting state dynamics must be bounded.

The implementation-side projection would be
\[
\hat\delta_m=
\frac{\hat b_q^TS\hat r_q}{\hat b_q^TS\hat b_q},
\qquad
\hat b_q^TS\hat b_q\ge\hat\beta_b>0.
\]
This is not interchangeable with the true projection. In particular, the exact orthogonality \(b_q^TSr_{q,u}=0\) does not imply \(b_q^TS(r_q-\hat b_q\hat\delta_m)=0\). The difference belongs in the closed-loop uncertainty bound.

A nominal fixed direction could be used as a deliberately approximate alternative, but then its mismatch must also be carried explicitly as an uncertainty term. It must not be described as the actual input direction.

## 5. Online signal-availability audit

The residual definition contains the nominal request \(u_L\):
\[
r=f_p(X,p,\dot p)+B_p(X,p)u_L-A_0X-B_0u_L.
\]
Consequently, a regressor omitting \(u_L\) is not generally sufficient to represent this residual when the input-direction mismatch \((B_p-B_0)u_L\) is material.

| Candidate signal | Role | Online status required | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(x,\dot x,\theta,\dot\theta\) | Plant state | Measured or state-estimated | Define sensors/observer and error bounds; do not assume perfect state access without stating it. |
| \(u_L\) | Nominal LQI force request | Known internally to controller | Include it in the regressor or derive a different residual target that removes its dependence. |
| \(F_a\) | Actual applied force | Measured or reconstructed from a validated actuator model | Needed to distinguish command from applied force and to analyse actuator tracking error. |
| \(M,m,l,I,b_x,b_\theta\) | Plant parameters | Measured, identified, or estimated if used online | True simulation parameters are not admissible controller inputs merely because MATLAB knows them. |
| \(\dot M,\dot m,\dot l,\dot I\) | Parameter rates in attached-body model | Measured/estimated, or avoided by a reformulated estimator | Numerical differentiation may amplify noise; any estimator/filter error must be bounded. |
| \(d_x,d_\theta\) | External disturbances | Usually unknown; measured only if a sensor/observer is specified | Do not put unknown true disturbance values directly into the controller regressor. |
| \(\ddot x,\ddot\theta\) | Acceleration residual construction | Estimated from sensors/model | Differentiation/filter delay and noise affect the residual target; include in the estimator-error budget. |

## 6. RBF regressor: admissible structure, not yet a frozen design

A minimally honest normalized candidate regressor is
\[
z=\begin{bmatrix}
x/x_s & \dot x/v_s & \theta/\theta_s &
\dot\theta/\omega_s & u_L/F_s
\end{bmatrix}^T,
\]
where all scales are positive, physically declared normalization constants. This candidate uses plant states and the internally known nominal request. It avoids feeding true, unmeasured plant parameters or disturbances to the network.

However, this five-component regressor is sufficient only if the residual is a single-valued function of these signals over the approximation domain, or if the omitted parameter dependence is included in a separately bounded uncertainty. With time-varying mass properties, two plant instances can have the same (z) but different residuals. A fixed ideal RBF weight matrix over (z) alone cannot represent both exactly in general.

There are therefore two mathematically distinct routes, neither selected yet:

**Route A — parameter-informed approximation.** Add normalized estimates of the varying parameters (and, if the chosen plant equations require them, parameter-rate estimates) to (z). This requires a credible online measurement/identification scheme and bounded estimation errors. The ideal RBF weights may then be fixed over a compact joint state–parameter domain, avoiding an unaccounted ideal-weight derivative term.

**Route B — state/input-only approximation with robust residual.** Keep (z) limited to measured states and known commands, but treat the unrepresented variation caused by hidden parameters/rates as a bounded approximation/disturbance term. This requires a defensible uniform bound over all admissible parameter trajectories. It may produce a larger ultimate bound and cannot be asserted from training RMSE.

The study must choose between these routes after parameter and sensing assumptions are specified. Do not silently train on true (p,\dot p) and then claim the network is implementable from measured signals.

## 7. Adaptation-law prerequisites

No weight-update law is approved in this note. Before deriving one, the following must be fixed:

1. The exact online regressor and its normalization.
2. Whether \(\hat r_q\) is trained from acceleration residual targets, a filtered prediction error, or a Lyapunov-derived scalar force error. These are not equivalent objectives.
3. The measured/estimated signal used to construct the target and the corresponding noise/filter-error bound.
4. The compact RBF approximation domain and the basis centres/widths.
5. Whether the ideal weight matrix is constant over a joint domain or time varying. If time varying, the Lyapunov derivative must include its rate contribution.
6. The weight-estimate constraint set and exact projection operator, including the inequality needed in the Lyapunov proof.
7. How the scalar projected force error couples to the augmented LQI state and the actual input direction.

For the two-output convention \(\hat r_q=\hat\Theta^T\Phi(z)\), \(\hat\Theta\in\mathbb R^{N\times2}\). Any update must preserve these dimensions and derive the sign from the closed-loop error equation, not from analogy with a generic neural adaptive law. A bounded weight estimate alone does not establish bounded plant states or forward invariance of the approximation domain.

## 9. Literature traceability

The use of a compact approximation domain and the distinction between approximation error and finite-network training error must be treated consistently with RBF approximation theory [Park & Sandberg, 1991; Lewis, Jagannathan & Yeşildirek, 1998]. Parameter projection and robust adaptive modifications require the exact operator inequality and Lyapunov derivation for the selected convention [Ioannou & Sun, 1996; Lavretsky & Wise, 2013]. Uniform ultimate boundedness and forward invariance require explicit bounds and a positively invariant operating region [Khalil, 2002]. These references support the general methods; they do not certify this system's assumptions or prove the proposed controller stable.

The weighted metric and the two-route signal-availability decision are project-specific design constructions. They are derived and qualified here rather than attributed to a published theorem.
